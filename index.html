The problem

33 councils. 33 portals. Hours of reading.

London planning data is split across 32 boroughs and the City of London, each with its own portal, and buried in bureaucratic language like Class MA prior approvals and Section 106 agreements. Manual analysis is slow, and by the time it’s done the opportunity has moved.

Fragmented

Every authority publishes differently. No single, consistent view of a city that’s changing everywhere at once.

Opaque

Dense jargon hides what a filing actually means for a high street, a rent roll or a development appraisal.

Too slow

Analysts spend hours on triage instead of decisions, and the signal arrives after prices have already adjusted.

The Development Momentum Index

One score for how fast a neighbourhood is changing.

What is the Momentum Index? A score from 0 to 100 for how much new development is planned in a neighbourhood compared with the rest of London. It combines seven official sources into one score: planning applications filed in the last 12 months (how many, how many new homes, office-to-home conversions, new commercial space), how fast local station use is growing (TfL and ORR), population growth (ONS) and resale price momentum (HM Land Registry). A score of 90 means more momentum than about 90% of London. Alongside it, price headroom shows how much cheaper an area still is than the rest of London, so you can spot momentum before prices catch up.
