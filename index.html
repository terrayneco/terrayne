The problem

33 councils. 33 portals. Hours of reading.

London planning data is split across 32 boroughs and the City of London, each with its own portal, and buried in bureaucratic language like Class MA prior approvals and Section 106 agreements. Manual analysis is slow, and by the time it’s done the opportunity has moved.

Fragmented

Every authority publishes differently. No single, consistent view of a city that’s changing everywhere at once.

Opaque

Dense jargon hides what a filing actually means for a high street, a rent roll or a development appraisal.

Too slow

Analysts spend hours on triage instead of decisions, and the signal arrives after prices have already adjusted.

How it works

From filings to a decision.

The product isn’t the data, it’s the interpretation of the data. Terrayne connects planning, pipeline and footfall signals to explain where places are heading, built on the Greater London Authority’s Planning London Datahub, which collects application data from London’s planning authorities.

The Development Momentum Index

One score for how fast a neighbourhood is changing.

What is the Momentum Index? A score from 0 to 100 for how much new development is planned in a neighbourhood compared with the rest of London. It’s built from real planning applications filed in the last 12 months: how many there are, how many new homes they propose, how many offices are being converted into homes, and how much new commercial space is planned. A score of 90 means busier than about 90% of London.
