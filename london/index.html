How it works

Seven signals. One score.

Each signal ranks an area against every same-sized area of London, so a 90 means more activity than about 90% of the city. Planning filings show what’s coming; official statistics show what’s already changing. Read the full method.

Reading a score

Every area gets a score, a verdict, a direction of travel and a data confidence rating.

Verdict
  • 80–100 Changing fast
  • 65–79 Changing
  • 50–64 Steady
  • Under 50 Quiet
Direction of travel

Each signal is marked ↑ rising, → stable or ↓ falling over 12 months, and every score shows its change over 30 days and 12 months, so you can tell an area that’s speeding up from one that has peaked.

Data confidence

High, Medium or Low, depending on gaps in the evidence: no nearby stations, or too few applications, sales or census areas. A Low score rests on less evidence, so check it more carefully. How it’s rated.

Price headroom

Not part of the score: how local prices compare with the rest of London. 80 means cheaper than 80% of London. Pair it with momentum to find areas that are changing but where prices are still comparatively low.

Does it work?

We tested it on the past.

We rebuilt the planning half of the Index as it stood years ago and checked what happened next. Only the planning signals can be rebuilt historically, and past patterns don’t guarantee future ones.

Test 1 · Population

High scores were followed by more residents.

We rebuilt the planning part of the Index as it stood in June 2021 for 171 areas of London, then measured each area’s population change (2021→2024, ONS). Median growth per area:

Score 80–100+5.3%
Score 60–79+2.5%
Score 40–59+2.4%
Score under 40+1.6%

60% of areas scoring 80+ grew faster than London as a whole (+3.2%), against 30% of areas scoring under 40.

Test 2 · Prices

On its own, momentum didn’t predict prices. Cheaper areas did better.

Resale prices, like-for-like by property type, from the year to September 2021 to the year to September 2026 (HM Land Registry, 165 areas):

High planning activity was not followed by faster price growth: lots of new homes can hold resale prices back. That’s why every area also shows price headroom. Use the score to find where demand is growing, and headroom to judge value.

Questions about the Index

What is the London Momentum Index?

A score from 0 to 100 for how fast a London neighbourhood is changing, compared with the rest of London. It combines seven signals from five official sources: planning applications, new homes and commercial space proposed, office-to-home conversions, station use, population and resale prices. Terrayne tracks 18 neighbourhoods in detail and scores every part of London on a grid of map squares about 3 km across.

How is the score calculated?

For each signal, an area is ranked against every same-sized area of London, so a 90 means more activity than about 90% of London. The seven rankings are combined with fixed weights: planning signals 55%, official statistics 45%. Where a signal has no data, the others are re-weighted, and the data confidence rating says so. The full method is on the methodology page.

Does a high score mean prices will rise?

No. The score measures activity, not future prices. Our back-test found high scores were followed by faster population growth, but not by faster price growth: cheaper areas did better. Use the score to see where change and demand are building, and price headroom to judge value. It isn’t investment advice.

How often is it updated?

Every Monday by 7am UK time, from new planning filings in the GLA Planning London Datahub. Station use, population and sale prices update when the government publishes them, from monthly to yearly.

Why these 18 neighbourhoods?

They are London’s main regeneration and growth areas, from Stratford and Old Kent Road to Wembley Park and Croydon. Each is measured within about 1.7 km of its centre. Any other London postcode can still be checked: the postcode check scores the area it sits in.

Can I see the score for my own postcode?

Yes. The postcode check on the homepage shows the score for the area around any London postcode, with every significant scheme within walking distance. It’s free 3 times a month, and unlimited with Pro.

What does data confidence mean?

How complete the evidence behind a score is. An area with no stations has no station-use signal, and one with few sales has thin price data. High means no gaps, Medium one or two, Low three or more. It isn’t a forecast.
